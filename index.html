Requirement overview

You must complete all three of the following at a minimum of 5 words per minute (adjustable up to 25 wpm in Settings):

  1. Carry on a 5-minute conversation in Morse code.
  2. Correctly copy a 2-minute message sent in Morse code.
  3. Send a 25-word written passage in Morse code.

This app is a self-practice / self-test tool. Requirements #2 and #3 are auto-graded here for practice purposes. Requirement #1 (conversation) is timed and logged, but — like most BSA advancement — a unit leader or merit badge counselor should observe or review the transcript to confirm proficiency before the strip is awarded.

Morse code basics

All Morse timing is built from one unit, the length of a dot:

WhatLength
Dot (dit)1 unit
Dash (dah)3 units
Gap between dots/dashes inside a letter1 unit
Gap between letters3 units
Gap between words7 units

Speed is measured in words per minute using the word “PARIS” (50 units). One unit lasts 1200 ÷ wpm milliseconds, so at 5 wpm a dot is 240 ms and a dash is 720 ms.

  • Learn by sound, not by counting dots and dashes. Think of each letter as its own little rhythm (“di-dah” for A), not as a pattern to look up.
  • Steady rhythm beats speed. Even gaps matter more than going fast. Clear, evenly spaced letters and words are easy for a listener (and for this app) to read.
  • Send each letter in one smooth motion, then pause a little longer between letters and longer still between words.
  • Practice in short sessions (10–15 minutes) most days, and copy by writing letters as you hear them, without going back to fix them.
  • Farnsworth timing: some trainers (including ARRL practice sessions) send each character at a faster speed, such as 15 wpm, but stretch the gaps between letters and words so the overall speed is slower, such as 5 wpm. That helps you learn each letter as a sound instead of counting dots. When you send, keep your own rhythm even.
  • Make a mistake? Real operators send the error signal (eight dots), pause, and start the word again.
  • Calibrate once. In the Calibrate & Practice tab, key A, pause the way you would between two words, and key A again so the app learns your rhythm. It is saved and used for the Conversation and Sending tests.
Prosigns (procedural signals)

Prosigns are short signals that run the dots and dashes of two letters together as one character, with no letter gap. Operators use them to manage the conversation. Tap a row to hear it at your current speed.

ProsignMorseMeaning
K-.-“Go ahead” — ends your turn and invites the other station to send. Used instead of saying “over.” (A plain letter.)
KN-.--.Go ahead, only the station I just called
R.-.Received / understood (“roger”)
AR.-.-.End of message
SK...-.-End of contact — signing off
BT-...-Break / new paragraph (same as the = sign)
AS.-...Wait / stand by
CQ-.-. --.-General call — “anyone listening, please answer.” Sent as two letters.
DE-.. .“From.” “BASE CAMP DE SCOUT” = “Base Camp, this is Scout.” Sent as two letters.
?..--..Question / please repeat (“say again?”)
Error........Eight dots: mistake — start the word again
SOS...---...Distress signal, sent as one run-together character

A sample exchange

Each side signs with DE (“from”) and ends the turn with K (“go ahead”). A short, polite contact might sound like this:

BASE CAMP: HELLO SCOUT DE BASE CAMP K
SCOUT: BASE CAMP DE SCOUT R HELLO K
BASE CAMP: FB UR NAME K
SCOUT: NAME IS ALEX PSE AGN UR QTH K
BASE CAMP: QTH IS CAMP K
SCOUT: R TNX 73 SK

In the Conversation test, BASE CAMP uses plain words (no abbreviations or prosigns): copy each line by ear, write it in the Notes box, and key a short reply of your own. It does not have to match this example — it just needs to be a real back-and-forth.

1. Five-Minute Conversation

The app will play short lines in Morse code — the text is never shown, so you have to copy it by ear, just like a real contact. Use the Notes box to write down what you copy, then key your own reply using the button below or the spacebar (press and hold for dots/dashes, like a telegraph key — you'll hear a tone while it's held down). The session runs for 5 minutes. Send at whatever speed feels natural; the app figures out your dots and dashes on its own. Before you reply, write down what you copied in the Notes box. It is saved with each message and cleared when you send your reply, and your leader will see what you wrote next to what BASE CAMP actually sent.
